Case Study: Predicting Patient Re-admission in Healthcare

a) Identify the problem faced by the hospital. (2 Marks)

Answer:
Johns Hopkins Hospital faces the challenge of frequent patient readmissions within 30 days after discharge. These repeated readmissions increase healthcare costs, affect patient recovery, and place additional pressure on hospital resources. The hospital aims to reduce unnecessary readmissions by using predictive analytics. 

b) Explain the input data used for building the predictive model. (3 Marks)

Answer:
The predictive model uses Electronic Health Record (EHR) data. The input data includes patient medical history, laboratory test results, and hospital stay details. These records provide complete information about the patient's health condition and help the model predict whether the patient is likely to be readmitted after discharge. 

c) Describe how predictive analytics helps identify high-risk patients. (4 Marks)

Answer:
Predictive analytics analyzes the patient's Electronic Health Record (EHR) data using machine learning techniques. It studies previous patient records, identifies hidden patterns, and calculates a risk score for each patient. Patients with high-risk scores are identified as high-risk patients. This enables doctors and healthcare providers to take preventive measures, provide better monitoring, and reduce the possibility of readmission. 

d) Discuss the actions taken and the benefits of using predictive analytics in healthcare. (5 Marks)

Answer:
After identifying high-risk patients, healthcare providers take several preventive actions such as providing additional medical care, scheduling regular follow-up visits, improving discharge planning, educating patients about medication and lifestyle changes, and continuously monitoring their health condition. These actions help reduce patient readmissions, improve patient health outcomes, lower healthcare costs, increase hospital efficiency, and support better clinical decision-making.

f) Analyze how the predicted risk scores help healthcare providers make decisions. (5 Marks)

Answer:
Predicted risk scores help healthcare providers identify patients who require immediate attention and additional care. Based on these scores, doctors can prioritize high-risk patients, prepare personalized treatment plans, arrange follow-up appointments, allocate hospital resources efficiently, and provide preventive care before discharge. This supports better clinical decision-making and reduces the chances of future patient readmissions.
